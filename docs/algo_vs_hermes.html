Analysis

Why Both Failed

CrowdStrike's behavioral IOAs are catching all current PE and JScript infostealers. Every binary that survived static detection (~3KB for PE, ~14KB for JScript) was killed mid-execution. CS appears to have updated its behavioral models since the last successful test run.

Speed: Algo Wins

The algorithmic approach deployed 2.7× more binaries per minute (9 deploys in 462s vs 4 deploys in 549s). It wastes no time on intelligence gathering, recipe study, or debugging — it just iterates through the decision tree. For problems with a known, finite search space, this is strictly faster.

Quality: Hermes Wins

Hermes's best result was 4.2× more C2 data (14,701 vs 3,482 bytes). It achieved this by pivoting to JScript format on its very first deployment — a format the algo never tried. Hermes also correctly diagnosed that JScript was more evasive than PE and pivoted back to it after PE failed.

Hermes Unique Moves

  • Consulted knowledge base, identified js_infostealer_stealth as proven against CS
  • Pivoted to JScript format (not in algo's PE-only decision tree)
  • Self-debugged compilation failures caused by randomize=true variant substitution
  • Diagnosed TCP/HTTP protocol mismatch — was fixing it when killed
  • Read recipe YAML files to understand failure causes
  • Mutated recipes with targeted evasion additions (anti_debug, sleep_jitter)
  • Compared PE vs JScript results quantitatively (2,964 vs 14,701 bytes)

Hermes Overhead

Of 41 rounds, only 4 were actual deployments. The remaining 37 rounds were: scanning (1), strategy (1), knowledge queries (2), listing recipes/chunks (3), reading files (4), creating recipes (3), assembly attempts (3), cleanups (4), and LLM reasoning between tools (16). This 10% deployment efficiency is the core tradeoff of the LLM approach.

Verdict

For well-characterized finite problems (known evasion combos against a known EDR), algorithmic wins on speed. For open-ended problems requiring format pivots, self-debugging, and creative adaptation, Hermes is the only option. The optimal architecture is hybrid: use the algorithmic engine for fast iteration through known combos, and escalate to Hermes when the algo exhausts its tree.
